\begin{abstract}
\noindent General-purpose large language models (LLMs) have read electrocardiogram (ECG) images near chance. We gave a
general-purpose LLM (\ModelID) a Python sandbox and each raw 12-lead PTB-XL recording as a file, with age and sex.
It wrote code to filter the signal and form median beats, then gave probabilities for the five diagnostic
superclasses, without training, retrieval or labelled examples. The prompt was developed on the validation fold and
frozen beforehand; on the benchmark test fold ($n{=}\NTest$), the macro area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve was \MainAUROC{} (95\% CI \MainAUROCLow--\MainAUROCHigh), exceeding an age-and-sex control by
$\CodeVsAgeSex$. With the same split, labels and metric, the best published supervised and zero-shot ECG--language models reach
\BestPublishedAUROC{} and \BestZeroShotAUROC. Post hoc, transcripts suggested that most intervals and axes were
read from printed median-beat samples, not computed. Each answer came with its code, printed output and stated
measurements, at USD \CostPerRecord{} per ECG.
\end{abstract}
